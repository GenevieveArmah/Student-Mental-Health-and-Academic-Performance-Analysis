Overview

This dashboard provides a detailed analysis of the relationship between mental health and academic performance among university students. It is designed to help educators, institutional researchers, and policymakers understand the prevalence and academic implications of conditions such as depression, anxiety, and panic attacks. The goal is to support data-driven strategies that promote student success, resilience, and well-being in higher education.



Data Source:

Kaggle — Student Mental Health Data Analysis and Prediction

The dataset contains self-reported information on depression, anxiety, and panic attacks, alongside academic performance (CGPA ranges), program of study, year of study, and whether students had sought professional treatment for mental-health concerns.




Summary Metrics:

Total Student Count: Number of students surveyed from the Kaggle dataset

Depression Cases: Most frequently reported mental-health condition

Anxiety Cases: Second most common, often co-occurring with depression

Panic Attack Cases: Least reported, but often overlapping with other conditions

Students with Multiple Conditions: Identified through the Condition Co-Occurrence analysis (students reporting 2–3 conditions)

Sought Professional Treatment: Only about 6% of students indicated they had sought mental-health support

Average CGPA (Students with Symptoms): Slightly higher (~ +0.17) than those without symptoms

Average CGPA (Students without Symptoms): Slightly lower overall

Year 1 Students: Highest prevalence of reported mental-health challenges (over 40%)

Year 4 Students: Lowest prevalence (under 6%)

Female Students: Higher reported rates of anxiety (82.9%) and depression (75.8%) than males

Single Students: Represented the majority of those reporting anxiety (54%) and panic attacks (70%)



Dashboard Structure:

Page 1 – General Student Overview

Provides a demographic snapshot of the dataset, including total students, gender distribution, program representation, and average CGPA.

Page 2 – Mental Health and Wellness Overview

Displays the prevalence of depression, anxiety, and panic attacks, along with the proportion of students who sought treatment.
Includes a Condition Co-Occurrence visual categorizing students based on whether they experienced one, two, or three conditions concurrently.

Page 3 – Mental Health & Academic Performance

Explores how mental well-being interacts with academic achievement.
KPI cards display the average CGPA for students with and without symptoms, while a clustered bar chart compares CGPA across conditions (Depression, Anxiety, Panic Attack).
Additional visuals analyze whether students who sought treatment achieved higher academic outcomes than those who did not.




Insights:

1. Students who reported mental-health symptoms recorded slightly higher CGPA averages (~ +0.17) than those without symptoms, suggesting that awareness and coping strategies may correlate with stronger academic engagement.

2. Depression was the most reported condition, followed by Anxiety and Panic Attacks, yet only 6% of affected students sought professional treatment.

3. Those who did seek help tended to have higher academic averages, indicating the positive impact of counseling and early intervention.

4. Year 1 students exhibited the highest vulnerability to emotional distress, likely due to transition and adjustment pressures, while senior students showed improved emotional resilience.

5. Female students consistently reported higher rates of mental-health challenges, reinforcing the need for gender-sensitive wellness programs.

6. Single students experienced greater emotional strain compared to married students, highlighting the protective role of social and emotional support systems.




Recommendations:

1. Expand Access to Mental-Health Support: Establish and promote campus counseling and wellness centers that are accessible and stigma-free.

2. Target First-Year Students: Implement mentorship, orientation, and resilience-building programs to help with academic and emotional adjustment.

3. Encourage Help-Seeking Behavior: Increase mental-health literacy campaigns emphasizing the benefits of professional counseling.

4. Adopt Data-Informed Policies: Use dashboards like this to monitor well-being trends, identify high-risk groups, and guide resource allocation.

5. Promote Gender-Responsive Interventions: Ensure outreach efforts address gender differences in mental-health reporting and support access.

6. Foster a Supportive Culture: Train faculty and staff to recognize signs of distress and normalize mental-health conversations.
